This is a 2009 review of Ubuntu 9.10, then known as Karmic Koala. It records my experience on a desktop with an AMD Athlon 64 3500+, 1 GB of DDR-3200 RAM, an NVIDIA GeForce FX5200, and a 200 GB hard disk. It is not guidance for selecting or installing a current Linux distribution.

## What I observed then

The release was easy to obtain and install, and its live-session model made it possible to try the system before changing a machine. On my hardware, the stock installation reached the login screen in about 25.8 seconds and loaded the desktop in roughly another eight seconds.

Most of the hardware worked without separate setup. The graphics card required more attention, while the wireless adapter worked with the initial installation. I also found the desktop theme, software centre, and bundled applications approachable for a general desktop user.

Ubuntu 9.10 included OpenOffice, Empathy, Ubuntu One, and early Compiz effects. Those details are part of the period. They do not describe the current Ubuntu desktop, support lifecycle, cloud offerings, or package set.

## Why I keep the review

The useful part of the experience was practical: a system is easier to adopt when someone can test it on their own hardware, understand what will change, and recover without guessing. Current choices need current hardware compatibility, support windows, security updates, and application requirements.
